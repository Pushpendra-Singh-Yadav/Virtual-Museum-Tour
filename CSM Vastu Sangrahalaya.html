About the Museum

The Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (CSMVS), formerly known as the Prince of Wales Museum of Western India, is a premier art and history museum located in Mumbai. Established in the early 20th century, it was designed by architect George Wittet in the Indo-Saracenic style, blending traditional Indian, Islamic, and European architectural elements.

The museum's foundation was laid in 1905 and it was formally opened to the public in 1922. Over the years, it has become a central institution for the preservation and study of India's cultural heritage. It holds a collection of over 50,000 artifacts, offering a broad spectrum of art, archaeology, and natural history.

Highlights of Our Collections

Archaeology Section

This section features artifacts from the Harappan civilization, as well as a rich collection of Buddhist and Hindu sculptures. The pieces range from terracotta pottery and seals to stone carvings and ancient coins, providing a comprehensive timeline of ancient Indian history.

Art Section

The Art section is a treasure trove of miniature paintings, decorative arts, and a gallery dedicated to European oil paintings. Highlights include works from the Mughal, Rajasthani, and Deccan schools, showcasing intricate details and vibrant color palettes.

Natural History Section

A fascinating gallery for enthusiasts of the natural world, this section features a diverse collection of specimens of Indian fauna, including dioramas of various animal habitats. It provides valuable insights into India's rich biodiversity.

Pre and Proto History Section

This gallery takes visitors on a journey through time, from the Stone Age to the Bronze Age. Exhibits include tools, weapons, and pottery that shed light on the early human settlements and technological advancements in the Indian subcontinent.
