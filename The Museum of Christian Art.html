The Museum of Christian Art

A unique repository of Indo-Portuguese Christian Art.

About the Museum

The Museum of Christian Art (MoCA) in Velha Goa, India, is a distinguished institution housing a remarkable collection of Christian art from Goa and other parts of India. Established in 1994, it is a joint effort of the Archdiocese of Goa and the Indian National Trust for Art and Cultural Heritage (INTACH).

Located in the Convent of Santa Monica, the museum's mission is to preserve, research, and display the rich legacy of Indo-Portuguese Christian art, which reflects a unique blend of Portuguese and local Indian artistic traditions. Its collection is considered one of the most important of its kind in Asia.

The Collection: A Glimpse into Indo-Portuguese Art

Indo-Portuguese Ivories

One of the museum's most celebrated collections features intricately carved ivory statues. These pieces showcase a fusion of European religious iconography with local craftsmanship, such as the delicate filigree work and elaborate drapery. Notable pieces include statues of the Virgin Mary and various saints.

Wooden Sculptures

The wooden sculptures in the collection are known for their expressiveness and detailed carving. Many of these pieces were created for churches and private chapels, and they represent key figures and events from the Bible. The use of local wood and techniques gives them a distinct Goan character.

Religious Textiles and Vestments

The museum houses a stunning array of religious textiles, including chasubles, stoles, and altar cloths. These are often made from rich silks and velvets, embroidered with gold and silver thread. The motifs blend European Christian symbols with traditional Indian patterns, reflecting the syncretic nature of the art.

Metalwork and Gold

A significant portion of the collection is dedicated to liturgical objects made from precious metals. These include ornate crosses, chalices, monstrances, and candle stands. The craftsmanship is exceptionally fine, with pieces featuring intricate repoussé and filigree work, often adorned with precious stones.

Paintings and Manuscripts

The museum also has a small but important collection of paintings and manuscripts. These works, often on wood or canvas, depict scenes from the life of Christ and the saints. They serve as a historical record of the artistic influences and patronage during the Portuguese colonial period in Goa.
