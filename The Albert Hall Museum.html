The Albert Hall Museum

A grand repository of art and artifacts, nestled in the heart of Jaipur.

About the Museum

The Albert Hall Museum, the oldest museum in Rajasthan, is a magnificent example of Indo-Saracenic architecture. It was built in 1876 for a visit by the Prince of Wales, Albert Edward, and was formally opened as a public museum in 1887. The museum's intricate design, including its ornate carvings and domes, makes it a key landmark in Jaipur's 'Pink City'.

Designed by Colonel Sir Samuel Swinton Jacob, the museum's collection was envisioned to display the cultural wealth of Rajasthan. Its galleries are home to a diverse range of artifacts, from ancient pottery and textiles to weapons and natural history specimens, offering a comprehensive look into the region's rich artistic and historical legacy.

Highlights of Our Collection

Egyptian Mummy

One of the museum's most popular exhibits is an authentic Egyptian mummy, dating back to the Ptolemaic period. The mummy is that of a female, and the display includes her sarcophagus, providing a rare glimpse into ancient Egyptian burial practices and culture.

Miniature Paintings

The museum houses a spectacular collection of miniature paintings from various schools of Rajasthan, including Jaipur, Bundi, and Kishangarh. These exquisite paintings, often depicting mythological stories and courtly life, are a testament to the delicate artistry of the Mughal and Rajput eras.

Pottery and Metalware

This gallery showcases a wide range of pottery and metalware from different regions of India. The collection includes intricately designed traditional earthenware, brassware, and copper vessels, reflecting the diverse craftsmanship of the country.

Jewellery and Costumes

Explore the stunning array of traditional Rajasthani jewelry and costumes. The collection features ornate silver and gold ornaments, as well as elaborate textiles and royal garments that were once worn by maharajas and maharanis.
