A masterpiece of modernist architecture, showcasing a world-class collection of Indian art.

About the Museum

Designed by the world-renowned modernist architect Le Corbusier, the Government Museum and Art Gallery in Chandigarh is a significant architectural landmark in itself. It was established in 1947 to house artifacts recovered from Partition-era collections from Lahore, Pakistan, making its origins deeply connected to India's post-independence history.

The museum stands as a cultural hub and a testament to Le Corbusier's vision of integrating art with public life. Its brutalist architectural style, characterized by exposed concrete and clean lines, provides a stark yet beautiful backdrop for the rich and diverse collections housed within its walls. The museum is a major repository of Gandharan sculptures and has a rich tradition of promoting modern and contemporary art.

Highlights of Our Collections

Gandharan Sculptures

The museum's most celebrated collection is its impressive array of Gandharan sculptures. These Greco-Buddhist artifacts, from the ancient Gandhara region, are a unique blend of Hellenistic and Buddhist art forms, featuring intricately carved depictions of the Buddha and Bodhisattvas.

Miniature Paintings

Explore the delicate world of Indian miniature paintings, with a focus on the Pahari and Rajasthani schools. These paintings, rich in detail and color, often illustrate mythological tales, courtly life, and portraits, offering a window into the artistic traditions of the 18th and 19th centuries.

Modern & Contemporary Art

The museum's art gallery features a comprehensive collection of modern and contemporary Indian art. The collection includes works by eminent artists like Amrita Sher-Gil, M.F. Husain, and S.H. Raza, providing a narrative of India's artistic evolution through the 20th and 21st centuries.

Decorative Arts and Textiles

This gallery showcases a diverse range of decorative objects, including textiles, metalwork, and pottery. The collection reflects the rich craftsmanship and cultural practices of various regions across India, from intricate embroidery to finely worked metalware.
