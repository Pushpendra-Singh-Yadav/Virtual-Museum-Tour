The Napier Museum

A stunning architectural marvel and a repository of Kerala's cultural heritage.

About the Museum

The Napier Museum, a captivating piece of Indo-Saracenic architecture, was established in 1855 and named after the former Governor of Madras, Lord Napier. Located in the heart of Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, it is a living example of a beautiful architectural blend of traditional Kerala, Mughal, Chinese, and Gothic styles.

Designed by Robert Chisholm, the museum's unique features, including its Gothic roof and minarets, make it a landmark in itself. The museum's collections are meticulously curated to showcase Kerala’s rich history, art, and natural heritage, making it an essential destination for anyone interested in the culture of South India.

Highlights of Our Collection

Bronze Idols and Sculptures

The museum is renowned for its collection of bronze idols and temple sculptures from various eras. These pieces are masterpieces of South Indian craftsmanship, showcasing intricate details and religious iconography.

Ivory Carvings

A significant part of the collection is dedicated to exquisite ivory carvings, for which Kerala is famous. The pieces depict mythological figures, courtly scenes, and daily life, all carved with remarkable precision and artistry.

Musical Instruments and Ornaments

This gallery displays a wide array of traditional musical instruments, giving visitors a glimpse into Kerala's rich performing arts culture. The collection also includes antique ornaments and jewelry that were once worn by royalty and common folk alike.

Sree Chitra Art Gallery

Adjacent to the Napier Museum, the Sree Chitra Art Gallery houses a magnificent collection of paintings. It includes works by renowned Indian artists like Raja Ravi Varma and Nicholas Roerich, as well as masterpieces from the Mughal and Tanjore schools of art.
