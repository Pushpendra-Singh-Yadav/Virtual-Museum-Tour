The Indian Museum, Kolkata

The oldest and largest multipurpose museum in the Asia-Pacific region.

About the Museum

Founded in 1814 by the Asiatic Society of Bengal, the Indian Museum is the oldest and largest multipurpose museum in India. It is often referred to as 'Jadughar' (House of Wonders) by locals. The museum's primary mission is to collect and preserve objects of Indian cultural and natural history.

Housed in an iconic colonial building, the museum has six major sections: Art, Archaeology, Anthropology, Geology, Zoology, and Economic Botany. Its vast collection includes everything from ancient fossils and a rare Egyptian mummy to priceless Buddhist sculptures and exquisite Mughal paintings, making it a repository of India's rich heritage.

Highlights of Our Collections

Archaeology Gallery

This gallery showcases an invaluable collection of artifacts from various Indian civilizations, including the famous Bharhut and Gandhara sculptures. The highlight of the gallery is the Ashoka Pillar from Sarnath, which features the Lion Capital that is now India's national emblem.

Art Gallery

The Art gallery houses a spectacular collection of Indian miniature paintings from the Mughal and Rajput schools, as well as works from the Bengal School of Art. The exhibits also include rare textiles, ivory carvings, and decorative arts that highlight India's rich artistic traditions.

Egyptian Gallery

One of the museum's most popular attractions is its Egyptian gallery, home to a 4,000-year-old Egyptian mummy. The display provides a fascinating glimpse into ancient Egyptian civilization, including its burial rituals and artifacts.

Geological Gallery

This section displays a wide range of rocks, minerals, and fossils, including a large skeleton of an extinct animal. The gallery's meteorite collection is particularly noteworthy, providing insights into the origins of our solar system.
