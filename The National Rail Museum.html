The National Rail Museum

Preserving the majestic history of Indian railways since 1977.

About the Museum

Established in 1977, the National Rail Museum is a unique museum in Asia that showcases the rich heritage of Indian Railways. Located in Chanakyapuri, New Delhi, the museum spans over 10 acres, displaying a fascinating collection of over 100 real-size exhibits of Indian railway history.

The museum’s mission is to preserve the legacy of a rail network that played a crucial role in shaping modern India. It is a premier institution for all things rail, from vintage locomotives and royal saloons to signaling equipment and railway memorabilia. The museum also provides a wonderful learning experience for children with its interactive exhibits and toy train rides.

Highlights of Our Collection

Patiala State Monorail

A unique monorail system that once operated in Punjab, this exhibit is a rare example of a working monorail locomotive driven by an external steam engine. It’s a major attraction for visitors interested in railway engineering.

Fairy Queen (1855)

Certified by the Guinness Book of Records as the world's oldest working steam locomotive, the Fairy Queen is a symbol of Indian rail history. This beautifully preserved engine once pulled coaches from Howrah to Raniganj in the 19th century.

Saloon of the Prince of Wales

This magnificent saloon car was built for the Prince of Wales (later King Edward VII) during his visit to India in 1876. The opulent interior, with its exquisite furniture and fittings, offers a glimpse into the luxurious travel of the colonial era.

Saloon of the Maharaja of Mysore

A beautifully designed royal carriage, this saloon belonged to the Maharaja of Mysore. Its intricate woodwork and elegant design reflect the high level of craftsmanship and luxury that characterized travel for Indian royalty.
